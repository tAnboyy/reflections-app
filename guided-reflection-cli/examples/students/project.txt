Intro
As its known, caffeine serves as a stimulant to the nervous system. From its benefits ranging from enhanced alertness to increased energy, it is no wonder how this became a popular drug worldwide. However, there have been lingering debates on its link to stress and sleep quality. In this project, I will be using the Global Coffee Health Dataset, a collection of over 10k synthetic records mimicking realistic patterns of caffeine consumption seen worldwide, to tackle if there is a connection between high caffeine intake and overall health.

The questions I aim to answer are:

Is higher caffeine intake more dependent based on gender or occupations?
How does the relationship between caffeine intake and sleep quality differ by gender?
How does caffeine intake impact stress and sleep quality across various occupations?

Data Cleaning:

By using dropna(), I ensured an accurate analysis that consists of only relevant information and no random zero values. pd.Categorical() was utilized in order to verify that the x axis labels reflected in a ‘least to greatest’ manner. In addition, the variable palette was also used to align gender labels with typical associated colors. For example:

df = df[[‘Coffee_Intake’, ‘Sleep_Quality’, ‘Gender’]].dropna()
order = [‘Poor’, ‘Fair’, ‘Excellent’]
df[‘Sleep_Quality’] = pd.Categorical(df[‘Sleep_Quality’], categories=order, ordered=True)
palette = {“Female”: “pink”, “Male”: “blue”, “Other”: “gray”}

Lastly, all graphs were created using a combination of plt and sns, both playing a role in labeling, sizing, and transferring the data over. For visualizations concerning occupations, I decided to utilize bar graphs as they promote readability to minimize initial confusion when it comes to a larger range of x values, unlike boxplots, which specialize in variety and outliers. Moreover, the latter was used for the gender-based graphs to expand on the less varied results by also featuring outliers and exactly what the least amount of coffee a respondent consumed was, overall providing more significance.
Ex:

plt.figure(figsize=(10,6))

sns.barplot(data=df, x = ‘Sleep_Quality’, y = ‘Coffee_Intake’,
hue = ‘Occupation’, order = order, palette = palette)

Impact:
Based on these visualizations, it can be interpreted that:

On average, there is a common correlation between caffeine intake and personal health, especially for participants 55 years old and younger
Across all age groups and genders, overall health is at its best with up to 2 cups of coffee a day.
Males are slightly more likely to still have excellent sleep at a higher caffeine intake
Overall, all graphs portray the consistent finding: Higher caffeine intake aligns with higher stress and poorer sleep. These findings can help current coffee drinkers become more aware of their own habits, and ultimately lead them to a better lifestyle. Moreover, researchers can use this information as motivation to explore patterns not originally featured in the original dataset, such as student course load, total work hours, and the time caffeine was taken. Limitations on this graph however concerns how the category for “Other” is both unclear for gender and occupation-centric graphs, nor does it entail whether the results were skewed if various groups were combined into a single one for each.
